Thesis

Australia, 2030

A strategy director at a Sydney consulting firm receives a client request at 9:00 a.m. By 9:05, an AI research agent has collected market data from hundreds of sources. By 9:15, another agent has benchmarked competitors and identified industry trends. By 9:30, a financial modelling agent has produced multiple growth scenarios. By lunchtime, a draft client presentation is ready for review.

Five years earlier, this work would have required a team of junior consultants working for days or even weeks.

According to the Stanford AI Index Report 2025, AI systems have demonstrated significant improvements in reasoning, coding, and knowledge-intensive tasks. These advances suggest that AI agents are evolving from passive tools that answer questions into active systems capable of conducting research, coordinating tasks, generating reports, and supporting decision-making.

Australia's consulting industry is particularly exposed to this transformation because consulting is fundamentally an information-processing business. According to IBISWorld (2025), the Australian management consulting industry generated approximately AUD $45.9 billion in annual revenue and consisted of around 94,910 consulting businesses in 2025.

This forecast argues that AI agents will fundamentally reshape Australia's consulting industry by 2030. The most important change will not be the elimination of consultants themselves, but the automation of many research, analysis, documentation, and coordination tasks that currently support consulting projects.

Consulting productivity is likely to increase significantly. However, whether these gains translate into higher wages, lower prices, or higher profits will depend on industry structure, organizational adaptation, and policy choices. The central question is therefore who captures the gains from that transformation.

AI Capabilities and Economic Channels

Unlike traditional generative AI systems, AI agents can pursue goals across multiple steps. The Stanford AI Index Report 2025 documented significant improvements in reasoning, coding, and knowledge-intensive tasks, suggesting that advanced AI systems are increasingly capable of supporting professional services work such as research, analysis, and decision support.

By 2030, specialized agents are likely to handle market research, competitor analysis, report drafting, project coordination, and routine data analysis.

These capabilities affect the consulting industry through several economic channels.

  • Lower information-processing costs
  • Lower coordination costs
  • Higher labour productivity

Evidence suggests that AI adoption can generate substantial productivity gains. According to PwC’s Global AI Jobs Barometer 2025, industries most exposed to AI recorded 27% growth in revenue per employee, compared with only 9% in less exposed industries.
